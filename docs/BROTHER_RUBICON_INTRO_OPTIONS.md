# Brother Rubicon: ten candidate show introductions

**Status:** All ten are proposals as of 2026-10-16. The producer has not chosen a permanent introduction. Do not assume that any version has been approved or put one into all episodes without a decision.

The spoken show title remains **“The die’s cast.”** It is rendered independently from the stylized printed brand **Th’die’s Cast**.

## October 23, 2026: new approved structure and preferences

These original drafts are **source material**, not individually approved verbatim as canonical. The producer prefers: **2, 3 somewhat, 4, 5 strongly, 6 strongly, 7 somewhat, 8 strongly, 9, 10 strongly, and 12** from the later twelve-option batch. Favorite territory: restrained mystery; natural, unselfconscious strangeness; meaningful numbers outside tabletop games; a human, slightly monastic personality. Avoid contemporary podcast-host familiarity or theatrical pseudo-medieval language.

Every new episode should have **its own short, different observation** and then say, **exactly**: “I am Brother Rubicon. The die’s cast.” The “I am” is mandatory. The one-line episode-specific gameplay instructions follow these two elements. Distinctive closing codas include small jokes and, preferably, unexplained remarks leaving a subtle disturbance. The producer particularly likes: **“I believe that was the last. I had been told there would be one more.”**

The first eight episodes produced to this structure are method-focused listening auditions, not commitments that the particular observations must remain permanent.

## Options to audition in language before recording

1. **The practical fellow:** “Brother Rubicon here. This is The die’s cast. I'll give you a number. What you do with it is your affair.”
2. **The old storyteller:** “There was once a monk who kept a bag of dice beneath his bed. But that is another story. I am Brother Rubicon. Welcome to The die’s cast.”
3. **The impatient monk:** “You need a roll, not a sermon. Brother Rubicon. The die’s cast.”
4. **The reluctant philosopher:** “People ask whether chance has a mind of its own. I advise against asking it directly. I'm Brother Rubicon. This is The die’s cast.”
5. **The hospitable host:** “Come in. There's a place at the table. I'm Brother Rubicon. Welcome to The die’s cast.”
6. **The keeper of thresholds:** “Some doors open only after a number is spoken. I'm Brother Rubicon, and this is The die’s cast.”
7. **The mildly subversive brother:** “Our abbot disapproves of gaming. He disapproves rather more of losing. I am Brother Rubicon. The die’s cast.”
8. **The teller of warnings:** “The old stories warn against drawing lots in the dark. They never explain why. I'm Brother Rubicon. The die’s cast.”
9. **The man with a peculiar occupation:** “I have spent much of my life saying numbers aloud. There are worse vocations. Brother Rubicon, at your service. The die’s cast.”
10. **The quiet theologian:** “The die may be cast. The deed is still yours. I am Brother Rubicon. Welcome to The die’s cast.”

## Direction questions for later

- Does Rubicon work better as a concrete **person with implied history** (2, 7, 9), as someone with **understated humor** (3, 4), or as a **keeper of mysteries** (6, 8, 10)?
- Should the recurring opening be **tiny** (3), **welcoming** (5), or story-bearing (2, 7)?
- Keep the method-specific narrated introductions in the balanced dice auditions **provisional**, since they are merely short technical explanations. The user has not selected any of these permanent character introductions.
