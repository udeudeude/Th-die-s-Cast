# Research leads, compatibility checks, and media rights

_Working research log, 2026-10-09. Sources are linked for future re-verification._

## CC0 in brief

**CC0 / Creative Commons Zero** is a public-domain dedication, not simply "free to listen." Its creator or rightsholder relinquishes copyright and neighboring rights as fully as legally possible; copying, adaptation, redistribution, commercial use and public performance are allowed without a CC0 attribution requirement. Still credit creators as a good practice. CC0 does not waive everyone else's rights, such as publicity, privacy, patent, or trademark rights.

- https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
- https://creativecommons.org/public-domain/
- https://creativecommons.org/reusing-cc-licensed-content/

Record **creator, original source, date accessed, specific asset license, any modification, and proof of permission** in a per-episode manifest. An entire website being "free" does not license every embedded asset.

## Famous people's numbers — research-only

A proposed special D20 could feature **real archival clips** of different known speakers saying values 1–20 (or larger ranges). Proposed sources/people:
- James Earl Jones (authentic low, resonant voice)
- J. R. R. Tolkien (archival readings / lectures)
- Tom Rosenthal's song **“157”** (a counting song; verify any source track and rights)
- Kermit the Frog, Sesame Street, and related counting material (rights and performer/character identities)
- Other speakers with suitable independently licensed recordings, including guests reading the numbers directly for this series

Not an instruction to imitate voices or to scrape copyrighted recordings.

**Rights:** extracting even a single word from an existing recording can implicate the recording rightsholder, and songs can have a distinct composition copyright; famous characters, trademarks and publicity/likeness issues can arise separately. Short duration is not an automatic fair-use rule. If public-domain archival recordings are proposed, verify *this specific recording* and the relevant region/jurisdiction (do not assume Tolkien archival recordings are public domain).

Research paths:
1. Seek direct licenses or permission to sample specific clips, and preserve written scope (redistribution, modification, monetization, territory, duration).
2. Commission guest contributions or volunteer recordings with an explicit reuse/license agreement; keep the "famous voices" as actually participating contributors.
3. Identify CC0 / public domain recordings whose status is documented, ideally with eclectic personalities.
4. Only after clearance, build a metadata sheet mapping each face to person, timestamp in source, attribution, permissions and editing notes. Keep a locally-reproducible production path.
5. If the intended clips cannot be licensed, do not publish them; use an original Lewis multicasting / voiced-character experiment instead, clearly not an imitation of a real person.

Helpful copyright references:
- U.S. Copyright Office, Sound Recordings: https://www.copyright.gov/engage/docs/recording.pdf
- U.S. Copyright Office, Sampling: https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/educational-materials/Sampling-Interpolations-Beat-Stores-and-More-An-Introduction-for-Musicians-Using-Preexisting.pdf
- Fair use case-by-case analysis: https://copyright.gov/fair-use/

## Tabletop Audio — listen/reference vs redistribute

Official about/license: https://tabletopaudio.com/about.html

- Site says ten-minute ambiences are **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**. License requires credit, is noncommercial and does not authorize distributing *modified* versions.
- Site specifically says **SoundPad samples are not to be downloaded or used outside tabletopaudio.com**.
- Creator's site discusses monetized podcasts/videos and potential separate Patreon-based commercial licensing; obtain explicit permission before presenting a Tabletop Audio track *itself* as a new Th’die’s Cast episode, looping or editing tracks, or using SoundPad material.
- High priority: research a **link/cross-reference to Tabletop Audio** versus direct republication. Linking is not the same as redistributing an audio file.
- Do not read a permission to include quiet ambience within a commentary podcast as permission to publish a standalone ambience copy.

Other leads for source research:
- https://freesound.org (license varies per recording; CC0 records potentially suitable)
- https://www.d20sounds.com/ (investigate license; free personal game use is **not** blanket podcast republication)
- Official game soundtracks, e.g. https://the7thcontinent.seriouspoulp.com/en/resources/music (downloads may be free while rehosting is not permitted)
- The Night Cage original score (availability for listening is not a redistribution license)

## Publication permissions checklist

Before any third-party sound appears in a public audio feed:
- Identify all applicable owners (sound recording, composition, voice/performance/publicity, characters and trademarks as relevant).
- Link to exact license/permission (not just a "free" tag).
- Confirm the intended use: standalone podcast redistribution, possible remixing, editing, monetization, feed syndication, offline listening.
- Mark credit and source URL in the RSS/show notes.
- Archive authorization details, or select original/CC0 assets instead.
- Reevaluate game-specific compatibility claims using current official rules.

No third-party recordings have been approved for publication by these notes.
