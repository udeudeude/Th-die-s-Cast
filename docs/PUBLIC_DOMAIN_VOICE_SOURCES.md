# Initial research: real recorded numbers from public-domain sources

_2026-10-09. Research shortlist only, not an approved sound library. No numbers have yet been extracted and independently verified in the specific files._

## What constitutes a usable candidate?

A real historical recording is not automatically copyright-free because the speaker died, the recording is old, or a US agency hosts a copy. Verify the **exact master recording's** US copyright/public-domain status and distribution terms; then locate a **clean, isolated, intelligible spoken numeral**. Write a manifest for each clip: number, speaker, original recording, URL, timestamp, legal basis, and any restoration/processing.

### Priority 1: Peter Yearsley — LibriVox (strongest practical lead)

- Reader catalog: https://librivox.org/reader/167
- His recording of the segments of Lewis Carroll's **Rhyme? and Reason?**, especially the eight *Hunting of the Snark* parts: https://librivox.org/rhyme-and-reason-by-lewis-carroll/
- LibriVox states its volunteer recordings are **public domain in the United States**, making this an unusually strong legal candidate.
- Yearsley has performed many public-domain texts, suggesting numerous utterances of cardinal numbers across different chapters.
- Next step: audit **the actual audio** to identify numbers one through twenty, especially complete isolated numerals; do not assume all twenty occur. Chapter headings contain ordinal/roman numerals, which are *not* substitutes for exact cardinal-number clips.
- Advantage: characteristic storytelling voice, recordings from a relatively modern microphone, clear intelligibility versus wax cylinders.

### Priority 2: Thomas A. Edison, the actual speaker — Project Gutenberg (legal lead, low numerical coverage)

- His voice in the 1927 *Recording of Thomas Edison* project: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10137
- Edison *Around the World on the Phonograph*, recorded ~1888: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10311
- Project Gutenberg marks both **Public domain in the USA**.
- Distinguish **Thomas Edison speaking** from **Edison Records' other artists**; these are not interchangeable.
- The transcript of the brief 1927 Edison audio shows *Mary Had a Little Lamb*, not an enumerated set of dice numbers. There is no evidence it supplies 1–20. It is a possible famous-voice cameo, **not a complete source**.
- Edison 1888 audio is historically interesting but fidelity can be too poor for gameplay; inspect before selecting.

### Priority 3: Edison-company recordings in the Library of Congress Citizen DJ collection (huge, rights-clear archive)

- https://citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov/loc-edison/use/
- The LOC reports these Edison-company recordings as public domain and available for unencumbered reuse. Thousands of reusable samples from its Edison holdings can be downloaded and evaluated.
- Its early *spoken* records, dramatic scenes and spoken introductions are more relevant than songs or instrumentals.
- This offers multiple *performers*, not necessarily the original inventor. Search relevant scripts for numerical utterances and then check actual sample timestamps.
- Record source credit even where attribution is optional.
- Not yet proven to contain every number 1–20 with usable articulation.

### Lower priority: J. Edgar Hoover (actual historical voice, **rights not yet established**)

- FBI-hosted recording of a 1964 LBJ/Hoover conversation: https://www.fbi.gov/audio-repository/hoover_wh_jackson.mp3/view
- His voice is real, but **government hosting does not by itself mean the original recorded call is a federal government work**. LBJ/White House material, third-party sources, phone call ownership, and underlying recording-specific rights must be researched.
- Candidate for a phrase/number from a verified government-source master. Neither clean numeric excerpts nor clearance have been checked.

### Other leads worth scanning

- LibriVox all-author public domain readers (Peter Yearsley first): https://librivox.org/
- American pre-1926 phonographs with documentary proven PD status, especially children's counting or classroom records.
- Library of Congress public-domain *Inventing Entertainment* scene recordings, including announcers, speeches, and dialogue.

## Immediate small experiment

1. Find a spoken **one, five, ten, fifteen, twenty** using 2–3 different readers, starting with Peter Yearsley.
2. Log timestamps, source recording rights, and voice quality; do not scrape large numbers of files without known candidates.
3. Audio-normalize extracted snippets to consistent perceived level and apply tiny fades.
4. Preview a *five-face collage* for clarity before undertaking the full D20.
5. Expand to 1–20 only after the first few are unambiguous and verified.

## Non-candidates without specific permission

James Earl Jones commercial recordings; J.R.R. Tolkien commercial/archival recordings; Tom Rosenthal's **157**; Kermit and Sesame Street character performances. The target word being a single digit does not itself clear the underlying recording. Revisit if a specific licensed or demonstrably public-domain master is found.
